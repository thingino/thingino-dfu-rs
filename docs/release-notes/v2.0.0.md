thingino-dfu 2.0.0 is the Rust rewrite of the thingino flashing tools: the `thingino-dfu`
command line, the `dfu-remote` daemon, the browser flasher at https://webflash.thingino.com
and the `libtdfu-android` library that thingino-app loads. It continues the C tool at
wltechblog/thingino-dfu and keeps its command line, its wire protocol and its loaders.

## What you get

- `thingino-dfu`: list, diag, bootstrap, write with an optional read-back verify, read,
  erase and reboot, locally over USB or through a daemon with `--host`.
- `dfu-remote`: the daemon, listening on IPv4 and IPv6, with an origin allow-list for
  browsers and an optional `--token`.
- The browser flasher: WebUSB in the browser, or a daemon on the network.
- `libtdfu-android-2.0.0.tar.gz`: the JNI library for arm64-v8a and armeabi-v7a plus the
  loaders, in the layout thingino-app unpacks.
- 34 loader variants across the T10, T20, T21, T23, T30, T31, T32, T33, T40, T41 and A1
  families, fetched from the gtxaspec/u-boot `usbboot` release when the archive is built;
  each archive's README names the U-Boot commit its loaders came from.

## Compatibility

- The wire protocol is the 1.5.x one: a 1.5 client drives this daemon and this client
  drives a 1.5 daemon. The one addition is a narration command that `-d` sends over
  `--host`; an older daemon refuses it with a note and the run goes on.
- The command line flags are the 1.5 ones. The output is the tool's own: progress reads
  `writing`, `reading`, `verifying` and `erasing`, and the completion lines are
  `Write complete` and `Read complete: N bytes`.
- A device is identified from three bootrom register reads. Nothing is uploaded to or
  executed on a device to identify it.

## Verified

- Every operation on the bench, with the archive's own binaries, on 12 devices across
  nine SoC families, and on T31X and T23N units for the release candidates, locally and
  through the daemon.
- The browser flasher against the daemon, and the Android library flashing from a
  Pixel 10 Pro XL.
- The Windows and macOS archives are cross-compiled and have had less hardware time than
  Linux. Reports are welcome.

`SHA256SUMS` lists every archive.
